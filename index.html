About

I'm interested in perception and autonomy systems — specifically in understanding why they fail out in the real world, and what it takes to make them more reliable.

I started out in mechatronics, and that shaped how I still think about robotics: a robot is never just software or hardware, and the interesting problems almost always sit at the boundary between sensing, mechanics, and computation.

During my Master's at Northeastern University's Field Robotics Lab, I built an asynchronous stereo camera calibration pipeline and dug into the failure modes of ORB-SLAM3 on a quadruped robot. I like getting into sensor logs and reading through source code until I actually understand what's happening — then validating that understanding against real robot data.

Outside of research, I've built projects across localization, SLAM, 3D reconstruction, motion planning, and reinforcement learning. Different problems, same approach each time: understand the system, isolate the root cause, and build something that holds up.

Currently looking for roles in robotics perception, localization, sensor fusion, and autonomous systems — building and validating robotics software on real platforms.
